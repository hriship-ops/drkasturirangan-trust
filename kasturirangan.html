1940s
to
1950s

K. Kasturirangan is born on October 24, 1940, in Ernakulam, Kerala, to C.M. Krishnaswamy Iyer and Visalakshi. He attends Sree Rama Varma High School, Ernakulam, and later South Indian Education Society High School, in Bombay.

1961

Completes BSc (Honours) in Physics at Ramnarain Ruia College, Mumbai

1963

Completes MSc in Physics from Bombay University

Joins Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) for his doctoral work in cosmic X-rays in Vikram Sarabhai’s laboratory

1969

Marries Lakshmi. They have two sons, Rajesh and Sanjay

1971

Completes doctorate in experimental high-energy astronomy.

Receives an offer to do postdoctoral work with Nobel Laureate Prof Louis Alvarez at UC Berkeley, but is convinced by Sarabhai to join ISRO instead

1971

Takes on systems engineering responsibility for Aryabhata, India’s first satellite

1975

Aryabhata is successfully launched by a Soviet Kosmos-3M rocket from Kapustin Yar

1976

Appointed Project Director for Bhaskara-I, India’s first experimental earth observation satellite

1979

Bhaskara-I is launched

Appointed Project Director for Bhaskara-II

The Aryabhata Project, a book he co-edited with U.R. Rao, is released

1981

Bhaskara-II launches from Kapustin Yar aboard the Soviet’s Intercosmos launch vehicle

Awarded the Intercosmos Council Award of the Soviet Academy of Sciences

1982

Receives his first Padma award, the Padma Shri, in the Science and Engineering category

1983

Awarded the Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award in Engineering Sciences

1984

Appointed Deputy Director of ISRO Satellite Centre

1986

Appointed Associate Director of ISRO Satellite Centre

1988

IRS-1A, India’s first operational remote sensing satellite, developed under his leadership, successfully launches from the Soviet Cosmodrome at Baikonur aboard their Vostok-2M rocket

1990

Appointed Director of ISRO Satellite Centre, overseeing development of IRS-1B, INSAT-2, and other programmes

1991

IRS-1B, the second of the series of indigenously developed remote sensing satellites of India, successfully launches from the Soviet Cosmodrome at Baikonur

His wife Lakshmi passes away

1992

Receives his second Padma award, the Padma Bhushan, in the Science and Engineering category

INSAT-2A and 2B, India’s first multipurpose satellites (providing telecommunications, television broadcasting, meteorological and search and rescue services) launch from French Guiana using the European Ariane 4 launch vehicle

1993

The first flight of the indigenously developed launch vehicle PSLV narrowly misses success due to a software error

1994

Takes charge as Chairman of ISRO and Secretary of the Department of Space

1994

Appointed Chairman of the Space Commission, a position he held for nearly a decade under four successive Prime Ministers

The PSLV achieves its first successful launch

1997

India’s first second-generation operational remote sensing satellite, IRS-1C, is launched by a Molniya-M launch vehicle from the Baikonur Cosmodrome

1999

He proposes an Indian lunar mission during a public lecture in New Delhi

2000

Receives his third Padma award, the Padma Vibhushan, in the Science and Engineering category

2001

A new class of launch vehicle capable of launching heavy communication and navigation satellites, the GSLV, achieves its first successful test flight, making India one of the six countries in the world to demonstrate capabilities for geostationary satellite launches

Elected President of the Indian Academy of Sciences

2002

MetSat-1, India’s first dedicated meteorological satellite, is launched on the indigenous PSLV at Sriharikota

Conferred the title of Officer of the Légion d’honneur, France’s highest and most prestigious national order of merit

2003

MetSat-1 is renamed Kalpana-1, in honour of astronaut Kalpana Chawla who was killed when Space Shuttle Columbia disintegrated on reentry

Following a midnight phone call nominating him to the Rajya Sabha, he steps down as Chairman of ISRO

2004

Becomes Director of the National Institute of Advanced Studies (NIAS) in Bengaluru

2005

Elected President of the Indian National Academy of Engineering

2006

Appointed Academician of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences by Pope Benedict XVI. He is the fourth Indian to be appointed to the Academy after C.V. Raman, M.G.K. Menon and C.N.R. Rao.

2007

Delivers a 29-minute speech in the Rajya Sabha in support of the Indo-US nuclear 123 Agreement

2008

Chandrayaan-1, the Moon mission he championed for years, is successfully launched

Becomes the first chairman of the Karnataka Knowledge Commission (KKC)

2009

Joins the Planning Commission under Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, with charge of Science and Technology, Environment, Forests, Agriculture Research, and Climate Change

2012

Chairs the High Level Working Group on the Western Ghats, constituted by the Ministry of Environment and Forests

Appointed Chancellor of Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi

2013

The comprehensive report on the Western Ghats is released by the High-Level Working Group which he chaired

2014

Delivers the convocation address at IISc, Bengaluru, at its first ever convocation ceremony

Awarded the Rajyotsava Prashasti by the Government of Karnataka

2015

Astrosat, India’s first dedicated multi-wavelength space observatory and a mission he championed, is successfully launched

2015

The Hesaraghatta campus of the International Centre for Theoretical Sciences (ICTS), an institute Dr Kasturirangan was closely involved in establishing, is inaugurated

2017

Chairs a committee constituted by the Government of India to develop the National Education Policy (NEP)

2019

The Draft National Education Policy 2020 is officially released

2021

His book Space and Beyond is published by Springer Nature

2023

The National Curriculum Framework for School Education, formulated by a steering committee under his stewardship, is released

2025

Passes away at the age of 84 at his residence in Bengaluru
