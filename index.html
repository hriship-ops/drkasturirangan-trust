Inspired by a life of inquiry

Krishnaswamy Kasturirangan (24 October 1940 – 25 April 2025) was one of India’s most distinguished space scientists and public intellectuals. Over a career spanning several decades, he played a pivotal role in advancing India’s space programme through ISRO and steering national policy—first as a nominated Member of the Rajya Sabha and later of the Planning Commission. He went on to chair the panels that recommended India’s approach to environmental conservation in the Western Ghats and its National Education Policy 2020.

In recognition of his contributions, he has been awarded the Padma Shri, Padma Bhushan, and Padma Vibhushan. His life’s work reflects a rare blend of scientific excellence, institution-building, and public service.

The Dr Kasturirangan Trust was formed to honor and continue his legacy, spirit of innovation, interdisciplinary thinking, and commitment to national development through knowledge and education.

Where Curiosity Led

In December 2007, Kasturirangan spoke in the Rajya Sabha on the India–US civil nuclear agreement, then among the most contested questions in Indian politics. He argued it on its technical merits, keeping in mind the country’s growth and available natural resources. Beneath his stand lay his conviction that ‘international cooperation in science and technology has always served to enrich and accelerate indigenous efforts.’

At 30, newly married and doctoral studies completed, Kasturirangan was torn between joining Louis Alvarez’s prestigious lab at UC Berkeley and the barely months-old ISRO. It was Vikram Sarabhai who pointed out that the physics intuition and systems thinking he cultivated from flying balloon payloads during his thesis was precisely what ISRO needed. He chose India and never looked back.

Kasturirangan was thrilled when Indian-origin astronaut Kalpana Chawla took her first flight to space in 1997 and devastated by her death six years later in the space shuttle Columbia disaster. “She fired the minds of a new generation,” he noted at a memorial meeting. He was behind the renaming of METSAT, a meteorological satellite built by ISRO, as KALPANA-1 in her honour.
